# 03 · Young Newton believed in atoms of time

- **Length:** about 85 seconds.
- **Hook:** As a Cambridge student, Isaac Newton believed time came in
  smallest pieces.

## Narration and scenes

| Time | Visual | On-screen text | Narration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 s | A notebook opens to a page headed "Of Motion" | Cambridge, around 1664 | As a Cambridge student, around 1664, Isaac Newton kept a notebook of philosophical questions. |
| 6–20 s | The words "least distance", "least progression", "least degree of time" appear one by one | "a least degree of time" | Under the heading Of Motion he writes: there is a least distance, a least progression in motion, and a least degree of time. |
| 20–30 s | An hour glass whose sand falls in countable grains | "soe many least parts of time in an hower as there can be Τὸ νὺν᾽ς" | There are as many least parts of time in an hour as there can be "nows". And these leasts have no parts. |
| 30–36 s | A grain of matter beside a grain of time | "as I proved a least part in Matter" | He proves it, he says, the way he proved a least part of matter. |
| 36–52 s | A dot jumps one cell per tick on a grid of space and time | One least distance per least moment | Then something striking. The smallest motion covers exactly one least distance in exactly one least moment. So how does anything move slowly? |
| 52–62 s | The same dot, now pausing between jumps | "stops & stays" (later addition) | A later note in the margin answers: stops and stays. Read that way, slow motion is fast motion with pauses, and his atoms of space and time fix one top speed. |
| 62–74 s | The *Principia* page; a line shrinks without end | "semper diminuendas sine limite" | Twenty-odd years later the *Principia* argues the other way. When I speak of the least quantities, it says, think of quantities always to be diminished without limit. |
| 74–85 s | Two words side by side: "momentis" (1684) and "particulis" (1687) | De Motu → *Principia* | Even the time steps change their name. The 1684 drafts give each "moment" of time a triangle. The *Principia* speaks of "particles" of time, shrinking without end. |

## Sources and status

- **VERIFIED** *Quæstiones quædam Philosophiæ*, Cambridge University Library
  MS Add. 3996, folio 92v, Newton Project THEM00092.par27 under the heading
  "Of Motion" (hd12): "That there is a least distance, a least progression
  in motion & a least degree of time"; "there are soe many least parts of
  time in an hower as there can be Τὸ νὺν᾽ς"; "These leasts have no parts";
  "as I proved a least part in Matter". "as there can be stops & stays" is
  marked as an addition in the TEI.
- **VERIFIED** Dating: the section's only dated entries are comet
  observations of December 1664 and a halo on 19 February 1664/5; the TEI
  header says "early-mid 1660s". The page itself carries no date, so the
  script says "around 1664".
- **VERIFIED** Principia 1687, NATP00077.par42: "cave intelligas quantitates
  magnitudine determinatas, sed cogita semper diminuendas sine limite"; the
  same in 1713, NATP00082.par48.
- **VERIFIED** De Motu NATP00089.par9 and NATP00090.par17 use "singulis
  temporis momentis"; the 1687 and 1713 Proposition I uses "singulis
  temporis particulis" (NATP00077.par45; NATP00082.par51).
- **INTERPRETATION** The top-speed reading (least distance over least time,
  slower motion from pauses): editorial interpretation, medium confidence;
  see [TIME_SUBDIVISION](../../research/TIME_SUBDIVISION.md#newtons-time-parts-and-boundaries).
- **INTERPRETATION** That the momenta-to-particulæ change reflects a move
  away from indivisible moments: medium confidence; no passage states the
  motive.

## Do not say

- That Newton anticipated the speed of light limit or Planck time. The
  notebook's minima are speculative atomism, and their ratio is a speed, not
  an action.
- That the *Principia* disproves the notebook. It adopts a different method
  and states it explicitly.
