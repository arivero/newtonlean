# 06 · Old physics is a limit with teeth

- **Length:** about 90 seconds.
- **Hook:** Physics class says: set Planck's constant to zero and you get
  Newton. The physicist Michael Berry says that limit is singular.

## Narration and scenes

| Time | Visual | On-screen text | Narration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 s | Two dials: c → ∞ turns relativity into Newton; ħ → 0 turns quantum into classical | New theory → old theory as a constant → 0 | The textbook story: every new theory contains the old one as a limit. Send the speed of light to infinity and you recover Newton. Send Planck's constant to zero and you recover classical mechanics. |
| 10–16 s | A crossed-out "extra axiom" label on the old theory | An unneeded assumption? | As if the old theory just carried one unnecessary assumption. |
| 16–32 s | Berry's list of six reductions; the first row glows green | v/c: "an exceptional situation" | In 1994 Michael Berry listed six such limits. Relativity to Newton works beautifully, a smooth series in v over c, and Berry calls that case an exceptional situation. Usually the limits are singular. |
| 32–48 s | A wave whose wiggles get faster and faster; then a blur as it is averaged | δ = ħ / S | Quantum to classical is the hard one. The small number is Planck's constant divided by the action of the system, and the wave oscillates faster without end as it shrinks. To get classical physics back you must add something: average over the wiggles. Today that averaging is called decoherence. |
| 48–60 s | Two arrows reaching a corner in different orders, arriving at different points | Limits that do not commute | Berry adds a twist: the classical limit and the long-time limit do not commute. Take them in a different order and you get a different answer. |
| 60–80 s | Newton's polygon refining; a gauge labelled "action per step" sinks below ħ | Newton refines at ħ = 0 | Newton's own construction has the same shape. He shrinks his time steps toward zero, and each step carries an action that shrinks like the step cubed. Put Planck's constant beside it and refining pushes you out of the classical regime. Read that way, Newton set h-bar to zero first, and only then refined. |
| 80–90 s | The polygon becomes a smooth orbit, then dissolves into a wave pattern | Old theory = a limit with teeth | Old theories sit at the end of a limit, and the limit has teeth. |

## Sources and status

- **VERIFIED** Berry, "Asymptotics, singularities and the reduction of
  theories", in *Logic, Methodology and Philosophy of Science IX* (1994),
  p. 599: six reductions with δ = v/c, Gm/c²a, 1/N, 1/Re, λ/a and ħ/S;
  special relativity "is analytic in δ at δ = 0"; "My main point will be
  that this simple state of affairs is an exceptional situation. Usually,
  limits of physical theories are not analytic: they are singular".
- **VERIFIED** Berry 1994, p. 603: "the two limits do not commute".
- **VERIFIED** Berry, "Singular limits", *Physics Today* (May 2002): "Young's
  “demonstrable” invisibility requires an additional concept"; "Nowadays
  this application of the idea that the average of a cosine is zero,
  elaborated and reincarnated, is called decoherence."
- **INTERPRETATION** Newton's cell action of order F²h³/m and the ratio
  ħ/S = (t*/h)³ with t* = (mħ/F²)^(1/3); refinement at fixed ħ leaves the
  classical regime. A modern reconstruction recorded in the
  [Berry background](../../research/action-arguments/background-berry-singular-limits.md),
  item c.
- **INTERPRETATION** "Newton set ħ to zero first": our reading of the order
  of limits, not a claim about Newton's intentions.

## Do not say

- That Newton knew about Planck's constant.
- That classical mechanics is wrong. Berry's point is that recovering it
  needs an added ingredient and careful limits.
