# 07 · 一尺之捶，日取其半，萬世不竭

- **Length:** about 85 seconds.
- **Hook:** the Chinese sentence itself, written stroke by stroke, read
  aloud, then translated: "A one-foot stick, every day take away half of
  it, in a myriad ages it will not be exhausted."

## Sources and status

- **VERIFIED** *Zhuangzi*, chapter "Tianxia" (天下), as transcribed on
  Chinese Wikisource (莊子/天下): "一尺之捶，日取其半，萬世不竭。辯者以此與惠施相應，終身无窮。"
  Some editions write 棰 for 捶. The same list contains "鏃矢之疾，而有不行不止之時"
  (the arrow) and "飛鳥之景未嘗動也" (the flying bird's shadow).
- **VERIFIED** (secondary, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "School of
  Names", supplement on the paradoxes): the English rendering used here, and
  "a version of Zeno's racetrack paradox"; Mohist Canon B60 "appears to
  present a solution", implying "we do not first cover one fraction and then
  another but can instead move the whole distance in one stage".
- **VERIFIED** (secondary, SEP "Mohist Canons"): the Canons were "written
  and compiled most likely between the late 4th and mid 3rd century B.C."
- **VERIFIED** Newton, Principia 1687, NATP00077.par42: "cogita semper
  diminuendas sine limite".
- **SECONDARY** Hui Shi's dates, about 380 to 310 BCE (Britannica as
  reported in search results). Check before showing exact years.
- **MEMORY** Zeno's dates, about 490 to 430 BCE; Alexander in Bactria from
  329 BCE; the Greco-Bactrian kingdom from about 250 BCE to about 130 BCE.
  Check before production.
- **MEMORY** A.C. Graham's alternative reading of B60, in which halving
  stops at an indivisible "starting-point" (端, duan). If used, it would echo
  the young Newton's least parts (script 03); check Graham's *Later Mohist
  Logic, Ethics and Science* first.
- **INTERPRETATION** The transmission question. The user raised contact via
  Bactria as a hypothesis. Search found no evidence of transmission, and
  the comparative literature treats the paradoxes as parallel. The narration
  presents it as an open question.

## Do not say

- That Zeno's paradox reached China, or that Hui Shi read Zeno.
- That the debaters were Mohists. The Mohist Canons answer the halving
  argument; the stick belongs to the debaters' list.
- "Mozi said it". The sentence is in the *Zhuangzi*.
