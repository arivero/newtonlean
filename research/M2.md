# M2 — finite polygon and conditional limit

Status: finite construction checked; limiting area theorem conditional on an
independently given curve/enclosure. Existence of continuous-force motion is open.

The active forward-construction route is now the defect between constructed
polygons, followed by position control and realization. See
[time subdivision](TIME_SUBDIVISION.md) and
[finite partition control](PARTITION_CONTROL.md). The end-kick comparison
already disproves exact nesting in its constant-force example: common initial
data and equal total time give a fine endpoint correction `h*k*a`. This
scheduling result neither assumes a limiting curve nor supplies a general
central-force realization.

## Separate source stages

NATP00089 par8–9 and NATP00090 par16–17 give Theorem 1. Both construct equal
triangles by equal time cells, inertial continuation and impulses toward S.
Their passage to infinitely many infinitely small triangles has no numbered
limiting-lemma citation. Do not insert one retrospectively.

1687 NATP00077 par44–45 and 1713 NATP00082 par50–51 invoke Law I, laws'
corollary 1, and **Lemma III corollary 4**. The latter is respectively par10
and par11. Lemma III's unequal-width argument uses the largest width and a
fixed enclosing height (1687 par5–6; 1713 par6–7). Those limiting obligations
are additional to the finite construction. Textual analogy across stages does
not create a cross-stage proof edge.

## Construction and imports

`Polygon.motion` recursively constructs successive vertex pairs. `extend`
continues an inertial segment equally; `kick` adds a displacement parallel to
the radius at the vertex. These encode equal-cell inertia and composition,
not a solved differential equation. Centre S and plane are fixed. In the coordinate realization S is the origin;
negative kick parameter means inward impulse. The area theorem also covers
outward radial impulses, but that extension is not Newton's inward-force premise.

The synthetic interface imports two specialized area identities corresponding
to [Euclid I.38](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI38.html)
and [I.37](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/propI37.html).
The oriented and degenerate extensions are our reconstruction. The concrete
integer-coordinate model proves these identities directly by determinant
algebra; it is a consistency example, not all Euclidean geometry.
The current synthetic area's codomain is Int, so arbitrary real-valued areas
are not yet covered. No unsigned-area conclusion is made without a consistent
orientation and non-overlap of the fan.

`central_step_area`, `all_cell_areas` and `swept_eq` derive equal areas and their
finite sum; no input field asserts equality for the completed polygon.
`equal_time_area_reconstruction` proves cross-multiplied area/time ratios with
positive common dt. Ratios requiring division also need positive cell counts.
Zero impulse and zero-area radial configurations compile as regression examples.

## Refinement boundary

`rectangle_gap_bound` proves the finite telescoping rectangle bound
Σ width_i (height_(i+1) − height_i) ≤ maxWidth (height_N − height_0).
Every width has the same maximum and heights are monotone on this patch.
Actual nonnegative widths, enclosing rectangles and a fixed total-height bound
belong to geometric interpretation, not to a theorem creating a curve.

`enclosed_gap_vanishes` transfers an explicitly vanishing budget to an enclosed
gap. `sector_ratio_reconstruction` transfers given inner/outer polygon ratio
limits to an enclosed sector ratio. Both are conditional; we have not derived
those inputs from a uniformly refined family of the recursively constructed
polygons. Area convergence alone does not construct a trajectory or establish
that impulses converge to a continuous force. That unresolved bridge is kept
open, not replaced by Newton's assertion of uninterrupted action or a modern
ODE theorem. No triangle-limit premise counts as a proved geometric limit.

Validation: both Lean targets compile; graph anchors and accepted proof DAGs
validate; theorem axioms are inspected. Sources, reconstruction and outstanding
premises are deliberately distinct.

## Finite contact diagnostic (22 September 2026)

`Polygon/Contact.lean` separates position contact from velocity contact and
prescribed impulse contact. Its restriction construction re-indexes an already
supplied sequence of samples; it does not construct samples at arbitrarily
inserted times. `motion_restart` now proves that restarting the actual
recursive polygon from its kth pair, with the shifted impulse history, returns
the original construction after k+n cells. Consecutive constructed pairs share
their middle vertex. In the integer lattice realization,
`latticeMotionCell_impulseContact` derives the discrete velocity jump from the
actual recurrence, and `latticeMotionCell_zero_impulse_velocityContact` derives
velocity matching when that impulse is zero. These are unit-time finite
differences, not derivatives of a realized curve.

`equal_swept_area_does_not_identify_next_vertex` gives two lattice motions with
the same initial pair and equal swept-area sums at every finite count, but
different next vertices. Their constant radial impulse histories are both
inward (-1 and -2) and differ. This refutes identification from those scalar
area data alone; it proves neither failure of trajectory existence nor
nonuniqueness for a fixed specified force law.

These are modern diagnostics motivated by the three source-local polygon
arguments, not new historical proof edges. No action quantity or universal
constant is introduced. Direct module compilation and scoped axiom inspection
passed; the final repository validation is recorded separately.

## Finite polygon-refinement strip (22 September 2026)

`Polygon/RefinementStrip.lean` defines the closed signed doubled area between
a coarse edge A-C and a fine pair A-B-C as
`det A B + det B C - det A C`. It proves this is the doubled triangle area
`det (B-A) (C-A)`, and translation does not change it. Its `Nat` defect is
zero exactly when this signed strip is zero, so oriented cancellation is not
misreported as a positive enclosure estimate. The result uses only finite
determinant/triangle arithmetic: no integration, derivatives, quadrature,
measure, or limit theorem.

`oneCellMotionRefinementCompatible` makes the common initial and final
positions of a coarse finite cell and a two-cell fine `motion` explicit. The
inward lattice example has a nonzero defect. Compatibility is a spatial
premise, not a time-refinement result derived from a common force. No actual
curve is assumed or produced. The remaining forward-Proposition-I obligation
is a constructed refinement family, possibly non-nested, with position control
strong enough to justify a trajectory. Proposition II has a different premise:
its converse may begin independently with a given realized curve; this does
not discharge the forward existence obligation.
