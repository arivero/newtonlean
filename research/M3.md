# M3 — contact, cubic enclosure and uniform partition error

Status: conditional local inequalities and rational uniform error theorem
compile; the general curved configuration and its sharp coefficients remain open.

## Source geometry and distinct quantities

1687 NATP00077 par31–39 and 1713 NATP00082 par36–45 remain separate. In 1687
finite curvature is specified in the scholium; 1713 also puts it in the statement.
Case 1 uses AB²=AG·BD from circle geometry; the limiting intercept AI must be
finite and nonzero. Case 2 fixes an oblique ordinate angle; case 3 requires
convergence of its angle. Neither justifies arbitrary oblique-to-normal
identification. The cubic corollary invokes the nature of the parabola; it is
not obtained from Lemma X alone.

`Contact.Quantities` separates tangent length, chord length, normal contact
subtense, tangent departure and defect area. Their equality or proportionality
is never automatic. In coordinates (x,y), departure from y=0 is y; the area
between curve and tangent is distinct from the area between curve and chord.
For y=kx² on [0,h], these are kh³/3 and kh³/6; the containing triangle has
area kh³/2. Thus Newton's two-thirds and one-third refer to different regions.

## Checked inequalities and the actual assumptions

`normal_subtense_bound` takes the named circle identity and a **positive lower
bound** D0 ≤ AG, and proves BD ≤ AB²/D0. It currently assumes BD positive.
Zero departure is a separate degenerate case, not a finite-nonzero-curvature
instance. The proof does not construct circles or establish D0 from a curve.

`cubic_rectangle_bound` takes departure ≤ K h² and a rectangle enclosure
A ≤ departure·h, with h positive, and proves A ≤ K h³. This is genuine order
arithmetic from a quadratic inequality; it is not a field asserting the cubic
conclusion. To replace chord by tangent, then tangent by elapsed time, the
respective comparison bounds must be supplied. No O(h³) shorthand is used to
hide those missing steps or assert a two-sided sharp asymptotic.

## Constant-force finite sums

All sums below are finite. Compiling identities in `Contact/FiniteSums.lean`:

- 2 Σ(i=0..n−1) i + n = n².
- 6 Σ(i=0..n−1) i² + 3n² = 2n³+n.
- Σ(i=0..n−1) (i+1)² − Σ(i=0..n−1) i² = n².

For a linear velocity diagram v(t)=a t, positive rectangle widths t/n give
lower/upper areas a t²(1/2 ∓ 1/(2n)). For the parabola y=kx² they give
k h³(1/3 − 1/(2n)+1/(6n²)) and
k h³(1/3 + 1/(2n)+1/(6n²)). These are written algebraic consequences of the
checked sums. `reciprocal_budget_vanishes` independently checks the rational
Archimedean step for any error bounded by C/n. No integration theorem is used.

With geometric area enclosure and an area notion determined by arbitrarily
close rectangles, these yield s=a t²/2 and tangent-defect area a t³/6 for the
constant-force example. The sums and reciprocal convergence lemma compile;
the *assembled geometric coefficient identification is not yet a Lean theorem*.
In particular an imported formula defining the desired area would not finish
this obligation. The written derivation is separated from that formal gap.

## Uniform N^-2 bound

`rational_uniform_partition_error` proves Σ errors ≤ (Knum/Kden)/N² from a
common local cubic budget on **every** cell. Nonnegative errors are represented
by numerators with a common positive denominator D, which may vary with N.
N and Kden must also be positive. Clearing denominators gives the exact checked
inequality; all finite families of nonnegative rational errors can be represented
in this way. On a fixed interval T, absorb T³ into the fixed coefficient K.
For example local C(T/N)³ sums to CT³/N².

A single initial scaling estimate does not give that common C or a common
neighbourhood for every cell. The theorem requires them and makes no claim
that pointwise finite curvature on an arbitrary curve automatically supplies
a uniform bound. Missing/zero denominator and missing curvature assumptions
cannot instantiate the respective theorems. A failed uniform-budget example
is checked with `decide`.
