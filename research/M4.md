## Manuscript identities versus projected numbers

The archived seven-page Gregory extract is **C44**, conversations of 5–7 May
1694 (*Correspondence* III no.446 pp.334–340). Its p.339 note 1 identifies
RS Gregory MS fols.68–69. The separate projected-edition summary is **C42**,
no.461 pp.384–386. Direct C42 text was not obtained. Edinburgh's catalogue
failed through both browser and curl; no missing passage is invented.

C44 p.336 explicitly records moving Sections IV/V with quadrature material
into a separate booklet. Its Latin and manuscript-identity note were visually
checked. This supports a proposed relocation, not a mathematical dependency.
Classical Scholia remarks elsewhere in C44 remain outside this proof chain.

The proposed-number map uses Brackenridge's publisher edition, chapter 8 and
notes, as a secondary locator of Newton's drafts in CUL Add.3965.6. Exact folios
remain unresolved. The graph consequently separates `proposed_dependency`
(editorial interpretation) from accepted `proof_dependency`, and stores
`manuscript_identity` separately from `proposed_number`. The graph is a partial
supported outline, not a reconstruction of every missing manuscript link.

## Verified actual-edition changes

The generated edition-comparison.md aligns exact passages, rather than matching
paragraph numbers across editions. 1687 Proposition VI's main proof invokes
Law II and Lemma X; time-area proportionality is implicit. The 1713 main proof
instead cites Proposition I corollary 4 and Lemma XI corollaries 2–3. The next
paragraph **explicitly retains Lemma X corollary 4 as an alternative**. 1726
retains those citations. The earlier tangent-departure statement is relocated
to corollary 1, not erased.

1726 NATP00087 par91 materially expands 1713 par93: the orbit may touch or
cross the circle concentrically while sharing curvature and radius there.
Do not silently identify that wording with 1713. The quadratic and cubic
contact chain retains the finite-curvature restriction and parabola import.
No complete edition-wide absence claim is inferred from a missing graph edge.

## Checked route comparison

For the symmetric constant-force parabola y(t)=a t²/2 with zero initial normal
velocity, the midpoint of endpoints at ±t has height a t²/2. Lean proves this
from the coordinate construction (`symmetric_parabola_sagitta`). Its chord
sagitta therefore equals the one-sided normal displacement in this specific
configuration. A separate geometry/area premise identifies the parabola with
mechanical motion; defining the polynomial does not prove that premise.

With matched s and positive t, `generated_sagitta_commute` proves
2s/t² = 8s/(2t)². `constant_force_generated` recovers a from the constructed
parabola. Newton's proportionalities suppress this calibration constant; using
full arc duration for one route and half duration for the other without the
factor four would be wrong. The theorem compares these precisely matched
quantities, not arbitrary finite-arc departures, sagittae or forces at different
points. For a general finite-curvature orbit, matching initial coefficients,
angles, force normalization and area-time parametrization remains open.
