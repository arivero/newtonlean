# Arg003 v1: the inverse cube bounds the domain of the finite-force premise

- Proposer: Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code), 22 September 2026.
- Verdict: **inconclusive; locates where an action scale would act, derives
  none.** Newton's own Book I makes the inverse cube the boundary of the orbits
  that stay away from the centre. For that law alone the boundary is an action
  value, √(m*k), which is system-dependent (Arg002). For inverse-square gravity
  the boundary is zero action.
- Layer: action diagnostic (GOALS.md). The passages below are comparison
  witnesses outside the approved I–IV target; they add no proof edge.

## Steps and status

1. **The finite construction needs a vertex away from S and a finite force**
   (checked, `modern_reconstruction`). `Converse.vertex_at_centre_degenerate`
   shows that area data fix no direction at S. `CentralSchedule.refine_velocity`
   shows that refinement control reduces to force differences between nearby
   vertices. The 1713 Lemma X qualification is *Vi finita*
   (PROP_I_REALIZATION.md). A decreasing power-law force is unbounded near S.
   So the premises that could justify the construction's refinement hold
   along an orbit only while it stays away from S.

2. **Newton identifies the boundary** (explicit, Prop. XLV Cor. 1). For a
   body leaving an apsis, a force that decreases more slowly than the inverse
   cube (or increases) never brings it to the centre, and it reaches a lower
   apsis. A force that decreases faster brings it to the centre or sends it
   to infinity. The inverse cube is the boundary case, treated in
   Prop. XLI Cor. 3.

3. **What decides descent** (`modern_reconstruction`, effective potential,
   unchecked). For `F = k*rᵖ` (attractive) and angular momentum `L`:
   - `p > -3`: every orbit with `L > 0` avoids S. Threshold action: **0**.
   - `p < -3`: descent depends on energy as well as `L` (a finite barrier), so
     the threshold is no pure action value.
   - `p = -3`: the barrier term is `(L² − m*k)/(2*m*r²)`. Descent happens iff
     `L² < m*k` (with inward motion), for every energy. The threshold is the
     **action** `√(m*k)`, the common areal-velocity invariant of Arg002.

   The inverse cube is the one power law whose centre-avoidance boundary is an
   energy-independent action. This matches its unique action-preserving
   similarity (Arg002, step 4).

## Assessment

This is the strongest classical structure found so far that an action
constant could attach to. In Newton's Book I, the domain on which the
finite-force premise stays applicable is bounded, for the scale-invariant
law, by an action. The argument does not produce a universal constant. For
gravity the boundary sits at zero action, so a positive floor there would be
an added premise.

Modern context only, never a premise: in quantum mechanics the attractive
`1/r²` potential (force `k/r³`) collapses exactly when `m*k > ħ²*(l+1/2)²`.
The classical threshold `L² = m*k` reappears with `L²` replaced by
`ħ²*(l+1/2)²`, and for `l = 0` the residual `ħ/2` protects weakly coupled
states from falling into the centre. The quantum constant enters at the
boundary that Newton's Prop. XLV Cor. 1 marks. Nothing in the Principia
supplies its value.
