# Arg005 v1: the short-time law, from hypothesis to the 1713 force clause

- Proposer: Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code), 22 September 2026.
- Verdict: **supports the location of the constant.** The law for the very
  beginning of motion changes status at every stage. It is first assumed, then
  proved with a tangent premise, and in 1713 restricted to finite forces that
  are constant or continually increase or decrease. Three new 1713 corollaries
  make it the measure of force. The finite enclosure behind its proof holds
  exactly under the 1713 clause and fails for a finite force that rises and
  falls. A nonzero action bounds the times at which the law can be applied,
  and the clause is where that bound enters. It fixes no value.
- Layer: action diagnostic (GOALS.md). It adds no historical proof edge.

## Steps and status

1. **Status changes** (explicit). Hypothesis (Royal Society copy); lemma with
   a tangent premise (NATP00090); lemma for a *regular* force (1687); lemma
   for a *finite* force that is constant or monotone (1713); measure of force
   (1713 Cor. 3–5).
2. **What the 1713 clause secures** (`modern_reconstruction`, checked in
   finite form, integer magnitudes and unit cells).
   - `MonotoneEnclosure.enclosure`: in the impulse construction from rest,
     impulses between `lo` and `hi` put the space described between the spaces
     described under the constant forces `lo` and `hi`.
   - `monotone_enclosure`, `antitone_enclosure`: for a force that continually
     increases or decreases, these bounds are the initial and final forces.
     The space is squeezed between two Galilean motions whose forces close in
     on each other as the interval shrinks.
   - `nonmonotone_escapes`: a finite force that rises and falls (impulses 0,
     5, 0) describes space 5, while both of its endpoint forces give 0.
3. **What escapes the clause** (`modern_reconstruction`). A force that
   changes its direction of variation inside every interval, however short,
   defeats the enclosure at every scale. Finiteness alone leaves the squeeze
   without its bounds. The 1713 wording names both conditions: finiteness, and
   variation of one sign.
4. **Where a nonzero action acts** (modern context, never a premise).
   - Measuring the displacement `F t²/2m` of Cor. 4 with a momentum
     disturbance smaller than the impulse `F t` requires
     `(F t²/m)·(F t) ≳ ħ`, that is `t ≳ t_* = (m ħ/F²)^{1/3}`.
   - The free propagator over a time `t` varies on the length
     `(ħ t/m)^{1/2}`. The paths that dominate the path integral are nowhere
     differentiable, with increments of that size (Feynman and Hibbs 1965,
     ch. 7). That length overtakes `F t²/m` below the same `t_*`.

   A constant of action therefore limits Lemma X and its 1713 corollaries to
   times above `t_*`.
5. **Inference** (`editorial_interpretation`). Newton's revisions isolate the
   one premise of Section I about the very beginning of motion, keep it outside
   the Laws, and by 1713 state it as a condition on the force, while also
   making it the definition of force measurement. The classical theory is the
   case in which the clause holds down to `t = 0`. A constant of action is what
   would restrict it to `t ≳ t_*`.
