# Arg006 v1: the two 1713 finiteness clauses bound one local action

- Proposer: Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code), 22 September 2026.
- Verdict: **supports that the scale is an action.** In 1713 Newton wrote
  finiteness into two lemma statements: the force of Lemma X (*Vi finita*)
  and the curvature of Lemma XI (*curvaturam finitam*). For a body of momentum
  `p` under a force `F`, the radius on which the force turns the path is
  `ρ_F = p²/(m|F|)`. The two clauses therefore concern one local quantity,
  `p·ρ_F`, which has the dimension of action. The limiting argument shrinks
  arcs, times and areas and leaves it fixed. Newton measures finiteness
  against circles, a comparison that uses no unit. The quasi-classical
  condition of quantum mechanics, `m ħ |F|/p³ ≪ 1`, is the same comparison
  with a unit: `p·ρ_F ≫ ħ`.
- Layer: action diagnostic (GOALS.md). It adds no historical proof edge.

## The Latin, 1687 and 1713

| | 1687 | 1713 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lemma XI statement | [NATP00077 par31](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00077#par31): *Subtensa evanescens anguli contactus est ultimo in ratione duplicata subtensæ arcus contermini.* | [NATP00082 par36](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00082#par36): *Subtensa evanescens anguli contactus, in curvis omnibus curvaturam finitam ad punctum contactus habentibus, est ultimo in ratione duplicata subtensæ arcus contermini.* |
| Lemma XI scholium | [par39](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00077#par39): *curvaturam ad punctum A, nec infinite parvam esse nec infinite magnam, seu intervallum AI finitæ esse magnitudinis* | [par45](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00082#par45): kept |
| Lemma X force | [par27](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00077#par27): *vi regulari* | [par28](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00082#par28): *Vi finita, sive … immutabilis … sive … continuo augetur vel continuo diminuatur* |

The scholium motivates the restriction by an unending hierarchy of contact:
*Capi enim potest DB ut AD³ … habebitur series angulorum contactus pergens in
infinitum*, ending *Neque novit natura limitem* (1687 *Neq; novit natura
limitem*). In 1687 the restriction stood only in the scholium; 1713 also puts
it in the statement.

## Steps and status

1. **Two clauses** (explicit, 1713): a finite force, and a finite nonzero
   curvature at the point of contact.
2. **One local quantity** (`modern_reconstruction`, dimensional; unchecked).
   A force `F_⊥` across the motion turns the path with radius
   `ρ = p²/(m F_⊥)`. Together the clauses require `p·ρ` to be finite and
   nonzero at every point where Lemma XI is applied. The squeeze of Lemmas X–XI
   shrinks arcs `∝ h`, subtenses `∝ h²` and areas `∝ h³`, and leaves `p·ρ`
   fixed, because it belongs to the point while the refinement acts on
   increments.
3. **Newton's comparison uses no unit** (explicit). The scholium compares
   contact angles with *angulis contactuum, quos Circuli continent*. That is a
   comparison of orders, valid for circles of every size.
4. **The quasi-classical condition** (modern context, never a premise).
   Landau and Lifshitz (*Quantum Mechanics*, §46) give the condition for
   classical motion as `m ħ |F|/p³ ≪ 1`. With `ρ_F = p²/(m|F|)` it reads
   `ħ/(p ρ_F) ≪ 1`: the de Broglie wavelength must be small compared with the
   radius on which the force turns the path.
5. **Inference** (`editorial_interpretation`). The 1713 revisions demand
   finite force and finite curvature at every point that the limiting lemmas
   use. For a moving body the two demands are one demand on the action
   `p·ρ_F`. Newton's text supplies no unit for saying *how* finite. Quantum
   theory supplies it, and Newton's two clauses become the condition under
   which its motions reduce to his curves.

Connection: on the inverse-cube circles of Arg002 and Arg003, `ρ = r` and
`p·ρ = L = √(m k)` for every orbit of the family. The condition becomes
`√(m k) ≫ ħ`, the same comparison that decides quantum fall to the centre.
