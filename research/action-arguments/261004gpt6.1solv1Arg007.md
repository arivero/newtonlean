## Checked finite result

At the impulse vertex B, let c=B+h v and C=c+h² a(B). This is the next
polygon cell after the kick at B. The existing end-kick cell first drifts to
B and then applies that kick; no convention is silently changed.
`Diagnostic.DeflectionPotential.deflection_triangle` proves the signed
doubled area `h³ det(v,a)`. `unsigned_deflection_triangle` proves its
absolute-value counterpart. This triangle is not D_mesh between polygon and
curve.

For a=(0,-g), V(x,y)=m g y, `galilean_potential_step` proves exactly

    V(C)-V(c) = -m g² h².

`galilean_area_potential_cross_relation` proves the division-free identity

    m g · doubled_signed_area = h v_x · [V(C)-V(c)].

When m,v_x are nonnegative and h,g>0, taking absolute values and halving
gives `m Area = (v_x/(2g)) h |ΔV|`. Thus tau=v_x/(2g) is a time, and
`m Area/h = tau |ΔV|` has action dimension. The parallel-force transverse
velocity is invariant for every finite schedule
(`ForceClasses.parallel_transverse_schedule`), so tau is fixed along this
motion. This is exactly the potential difference at C versus c, rather than
the potential change along an equal-potential orbit.

For a(B)=-w B and V(p)=m w |p|²/2, `harmonic_potential_step` instead proves

    ΔV = -m w² h² |B|² - m w² h³ (B·v) + m w³ h⁴ |B|²/2.

The first term is `-m |a(B)|² h²`. Its identification with the whole finite
potential change is refuted by `harmonic_leading_term_not_exact`: at
m=w=1, h=1/2, B=(1,0), v=(0,1), the step is -7/32 rather than -1/4.
Compilation establishes these finite arithmetic statements; curve limits
and physical interpretation remain separate.

On a uniform circular central orbit, `|a|=v²/r` gives tau=r/(2v).
For a distance-only law f(r)=|a| and **specific** angular momentum
ell=r v_perp, tau=ell/(2 r f(r)). If L denotes angular momentum including
mass, replace ell by L/m. For ell≠0 and nonzero force on a varying-radius
orbit, tau is constant precisely when r f(r) is constant on the radii
visited, not necessarily on the law's entire domain. Radial motion has
ell=0 and tau=0 for arbitrary f; it is an exception to the proposed
unqualified “exactly when f∝1/r” statement. Direction-dependent laws need
the corresponding condition along the actual path.

For a differentiable planar force on a simply connected local region,
the rotated-work one-form is `F_y dx-F_x dy`. Equality of mixed partials
requires `div F=0`. Distance-only inward fields then satisfy
`(r f(r))'=0`; direction-dependent `f=c(theta)/r` also qualifies. A local
primitive is an angular potential. Its global single-valuedness additionally
requires zero circulation; a positive nonzero inward c has nonzero mean and
a multivalued primitive around S. This diagnostic calculation has not been
formalized and is not a supplied proof dependency or an existence result for
our completed trajectory.

The actual Newton end-kick polygon need not interpolate points on the
