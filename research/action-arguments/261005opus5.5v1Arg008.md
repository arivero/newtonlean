# Arg008 v1: Δt·ΔV is the action of the deflection, and ħ meets it at one place

- Proposer: Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code), 5 October 2026, from a discussion
  with the user.
- Verdict: **supports the location and kind; the value is supplied from
  outside.** In Newton's cell, the user's object `Δt·ΔV`, with `ΔV` taken
  between the actual point and the inertial continuation, equals the impulse
  times the departure from the tangent, `F²Δt³/m`, an action. Classical
  existence needs some scale but leaves its value free. Quantum mechanics
  supplies `ħ`, and Newton's limit `h → 0` at fixed force carries this
  per-cell action through `ħ` at `t_* = (mħ/F²)^{1/3}`, the time already found
  in Arg005. Nothing in the classical construction selects that value.
- Layer: action diagnostic (GOALS.md). It adds no historical proof edge and
  supplies no premise to an edition-local theorem.

## Steps and status

1. **Newton's cell.** With the kick at B and step `h`: `c = B + v h` (Law I)
   and `C = c + a h²` (the deflection along the force at B). On the
   constructed curve the departure from the tangent is `a h²/2`.
   *Status:* `modern_reconstruction`; the cell is the repository's end-kick
   recurrence.

2. **Deflection triangle and potential step.** Doubled area of `B, c, C` is
   `h³ det(v, a)`, and for the uniform field `V = m g y`,
   `V(C) − V(c) = −m g² h²` exactly. On the constructed curves the
   normalized triangle tends to `det(v, a)/2`, and for the harmonic field
   `ΔV/h²` tends to `−m |a|²/2`.
   *Status:* checked in Lean: `DeflectionPotential.deflection_triangle`,
   `galilean_potential_step`, `galilean_area_potential_cross_relation`,
   `harmonic_potential_step`;
   `GeneralForceTangentTriangle.normalized_triangles_converge`;
   `ConstructedHarmonicPotential.normalized_potential_steps_converge`.

3. **Area against potential.** At leading order `m·Area = τ·Δt·|ΔV|` with
   `τ = v⊥/(2|a|)`, a time (`v⊥` is the velocity component across the force).
   For uniform gravity `τ = v₀/(2g)`, exact on every polygon cell and constant
   along the parabola. For a circle under any law, `τ = r/(2v)`. For a
   general central orbit `τ = L/(2 r |f(r)|)`, constant along non-circular
   orbits exactly when `f ∝ 1/r`. The planar test that `Δt·(Δx×F)` is
   `Δt·ΔW` for a local potential `W` (true exactly when `div F = 0`) singles
   out the same law, with `W` a multiple of the polar angle.
   *Status:* the Galilean case is Lean-checked (step 2); the rest is
   derivation.

4. **The new identification.** `Δt·|ΔV| ≈ |Δp|·|δx|`, with impulse
   `Δp = F Δt` and departure `δx = F Δt²/m`, so `Δt·|ΔV| ≈ F² Δt³/m`. Exact
   for uniform gravity. The user's object is the action carried by the
   deflection: impulse times sagitta.
   *Status:* derivation; exact Galilean case by step 2.

5. **Scales classical existence needs.** A Lipschitz bound `L` defines the
   local dynamical time `τ_L = 1/√L` (harmonic `1/ω`; gravity about
   `√(r³/GM)`). Peano's theorem needs only a force bound, and gives existence
   along a subsequence without uniqueness or a constructive rate. Newton's
   1713 Lemma X premise (*Vi finita*, monotone along the path) is a bound at
   that level. The current estimates also fix a unit of time implicitly; see
   [scales and units](../GENERAL_FORCE_DESIGN.md#scales-and-units). Every one
   of these scales depends on the system and rescales with the unit of time.
   *Status:* derivation; the non-constructiveness of Peano's theorem is
   cited from memory (Aberth 1971; Pour-El and Richards 1979) and needs
   checking.

6. **Where quantum mechanics enters.** The classical regime needs
   `S/ħ ≫ 1` and a slowly varying force in both directions:
   `ħ m |F_∥|/p³ ≪ 1` along the path (WKB) and `ħ m |F_⊥|/p³ ≪ 1` across it
   (Arg006's `p·ρ_F ≫ ħ`). These have the same form with different force
   components; Arg006 states them as one. A deflection and the impulse that
   produces it are both sharp only when `F² Δt³/m ≳ ħ`, which gives
   `Δt ≳ t_* = (mħ/F²)^{1/3}`, Arg005's bound, now reached from `Δt·ΔV`.
   *Status:* derivation.

7. **An experimental anchor.** For uniform gravity, `F = m g` gives
   `t_* = (ħ/(m g²))^{1/3}` and length `(ħ²/(m² g))^{1/3}`. For neutrons
   these are about 0.9 ms and 7.4 μm. Dividing the length by `2^{1/3}` gives
   5.9 μm, the scale of the gravitational quantum states of neutrons measured
   by Nesvizhevsky et al. (*Nature*, 2002).
   *Status:* arithmetic checked here; the citation is from memory and needs
   checking.

## Consequence

Newton's polygon limit has a physical floor exactly where the user's object
becomes an action of order `ħ`. The classical mathematics cannot see the
floor, because its scales are free; the same holds for every scale found in
steps 3 and 5. This locates the hypothesis precisely and leaves its value
external, in agreement with Arg001's finding that no checked classical residual
selects a constant.
