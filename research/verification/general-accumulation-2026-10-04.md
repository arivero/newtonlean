# Actual finite refinement accumulation, 4 October 2026

Status: checked finite rational estimate, `modern_reconstruction`. No historical dependency or trajectory existence is asserted. The source-first Proposition I programme remains in `research/GOALS.md`.

`BarrowLib/Polygon/FiniteAccumulation.lean` defines the actual recurrences `coarseAt b h s n` (one cell of duration `h+h` per block) and `fineAt a h s n` (two cells of duration `h` per block) for arbitrary rational point maps `a,b`. `cross_block_error` combines a triangle inequality, cross-map propagation through both half cells, and the local coarse-map defect already derived in `FiniteEstimates`; `cross_actual_error_le_budget` inducts over the actual maps. The old same-map public names are corollaries obtained by setting `a=b`. There is no supplied adjacent-error or Cauchy field. `FiniteGrowth` was moved to `BarrowLib/Common` with the old Newton module as a compatibility import.

The exact premises of `cross_actual_uniform_error` are `h ≥ 0`, `L,E,B,V ≥ 0`, `T = 2nh`, `T(1+L) ≤ 1/2`, the global cross contract `|a(p)-b(q)|₁ ≤ L|p-q|₁ + E`, the local coarse contract `|b(p)-b(q)|₁ ≤ L|p-q|₁ + E`, a bound `B` on `b` at the first-half arrival of each actual coarse state indexed `k<n`, and a bound `V` on the actual coarse velocity at each `k<n`. The same `E` may overbound both map discrepancy and coarse-sample regularity error. A region-local version still needs membership of the cross-map sampled arrivals at actual fine and counterfactual coarse cells, plus the two coarse local-defect arrival pairs; bounds only at actual coarse endpoints are insufficient. Centrality, inward direction, a potential, and convergence are not premises of this arithmetic theorem; Newton-side applications must impose the appropriate force class separately.

With `H=|h|` and `K=(1+H)(1+HL)`, the proved Lean conclusion is `D_n ≤ 2n S`, where `D_n` is the coordinate L1 distance between actual fine and coarse endpoints and `S` is the *defined* `uniformBlockSource`. Expanding its definition in ordinary rational arithmetic gives `S = H²(B+LV+HLB)+(K+3)HE`. Thus for nonnegative `h`, the displayed bound reads `D_n ≤ T h(B+LV+hLB)+T(K+3)E`. The small-window premise bounds the finite power of `K²` by 2 through four explicit nonnegative factors per block and `FiniteGrowth.uniform_amplification`. For `n≥1`, elementary inequalities give `K≤2` and `h≤T/2`, so the estimate has the requested scale `O(T h(B+LV+TLB)+T E)`. The simplified constant-5 formula is an algebraic reading of the Lean bound; it is not separately named as a Lean theorem.

Exact rational controls compile: zero blocks agree for every map, the first block at zero duration agrees, and a constant downward map with `L=E=0`, zero initial state and `h=1/2` has distance exactly `1/4`, matching its first-block budget. The distinct constant maps `a=(0,-1)` and `b=(0,-2)` satisfy the cross contract with `L=0,E=1`; their first-block fine/coarse distance at `h=1/2` is exactly `5/4`. `cross_constant_requires_sample_error` proves that this exceeds the coarse local budget calculated with `E=0` (which is `1/2`), so a cross-map proof cannot erase the sample discrepancy. The existing `FiniteEstimates.unequal_sample_control_nonzero` checks the one-cell analogue.

No `sorry` was added. The theorem compares distinct finite sample levels but does not construct a Cauchy family, a curve, partition independence, or historical Proposition I closure.
